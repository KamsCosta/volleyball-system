The story

The road to the court

Tifanny Pereira de Abreu was born on October 29, 1984, in Paraíso do Tocantins, a small town in the Brazilian countryside, the youngest of seven siblings. At 17 she found in volleyball a place where she felt welcome, even before her transition and even more so after it.

In 2012, already playing in Europe, Tifanny realized she needed to live her gender transition. Moving to the continent also made access to medical care easier. The name Tifanny, which she had already been using for a few years, was chosen in honor of a friend from a group of LGBT volleyball players.

Her debut in women's volleyball, playing in Italy, was breathtaking: 28 points and the player of the match award in her team's 3-1 win. But the performance also opened a public debate. The opposing team's captain and coach publicly questioned whether there was a competitive advantage, a discussion that would follow her career in the years to come.

In 2017, when she signed with Sesi Vôlei Bauru, Tifanny became the first trans athlete to compete in a top-level championship in Brazilian volleyball. In her first three Superliga matches she scored 70 points, the best average in the competition. In 2018 she established herself as one of the league's stars, peaking at 39 points in a single match against the then unbeaten leader Praia Clube.

Since 2021 she has worn the Osasco jersey, one of the most traditional and successful clubs in Brazil. In just four months she was the top scorer of the first phase of the 2021/22 Superliga, with 225 points. Today she describes the support of the coaching staff, her teammates and the fans as one of the high points of her career.

Why it matters

About transphobia in sport

Trans athletes often face public scrutiny, questions about their legitimacy and, too often, hostile speech on and off the court. Tifanny's career, marked by undeniable technical achievements, has also been marked by this kind of debate since her debut.

Being the "first" was never a goal. It was a consequence of simply wanting to play the sport she loves. Still, this pioneering role brought a visibility that opened the way for the discussion about inclusion in Brazilian sport.

This system supports the right of every person, cis or trans, to play the sport they love, to be called by the name they chose and to be treated with respect and dignity, on and off the court.
